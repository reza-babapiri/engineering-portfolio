Planet Carrier
CAD & Static FEA

From a SOLIDWORKS planetary gearbox assembly to an ANSYS study of a stress-critical output-shaft groove, local geometric refinement, and a documented bearing-load reaction audit.

01 / ENGINEERING QUESTION

Stress concentration at the groove

A circumferential transition in the carrier output shaft was identified as a critical region. A nominal R0.2 mm internal root fillet was introduced for a revised geometry study. The investigation assessed equivalent stress, displacement, local mesh sensitivity, and force/moment reactions under simplified planet-pin loading.

02 / SOLIDWORKS & GEOMETRY

Assembly and local model revision

The supplied SOLIDWORKS video documents an exploded digital planetary gearbox assembly; the ANSYS screenshots document the isolated carrier and output shaft. The selected model includes a circular flange, three planet-pin bores and an output-shaft section with a longitudinal slot. The groove-root internal corners were revised to nominal R0.2 mm radii while preserving the primary form.

Video animation demonstrates the CAD assembly presentation rather than validated gear meshing or mechanical performance. Manufacturing drawings, gear module, backlash specification and detailed mating tolerances are outside the verified evidence for this case.

03 / FEM MODEL

Boundary conditions and material

ANSYS Mechanical 2025 R2 Student was used for a preliminary static structural analysis. The nominal material was Structural Steel (E ≈ 200 GPa; ν = 0.30; tensile yield strength 250 MPa). A Cylindrical Support represented an idealized shaft restraint, with an applied torque of approximately +8180 N·mm about the model's global Y axis.

04 / MESH EVIDENCE

Targeted refinement and stress sensitivity

The local mesh around the groove was refined in stages, with a later refinement around the Planet B bore. Quadratic Tet10 elements were reported. Final recorded quality statistics: 91,834 nodes, 54,860 elements, mean Element Quality 0.69311, minimum 0.096109.

06 / LOAD REACTION AUDIT

Explaining the residual support force

Isolating planet loads showed A and C matching their nominal applied forces (121.18 N and 121.19 N to displayed precision). Planet B originally gave a reaction magnitude of 119.22 N against the entered 121.18 N, then 121.87 N after extra local bore refinement. The final combined case gave 0.68293 N support reaction. This shows sensitivity to the discretized bearing-load transfer, not evidence of a solver failure.

The final Y reaction moment was −8187.2 N·mm, close in magnitude to +8180 N·mm applied torque. Because off-axis planet loads contribute moments, this numerical proximity is not itself a full moment-equilibrium audit.

07 / LIMITATIONS AND NEXT WORK

Clear boundaries of the claim

The value 2.37 is only the selected 250 MPa yield value divided by the reported 105.40 MPa peak. The study does not establish real gear tooth contact loads, load-sharing variation, bearing compliance, pin-bore contact, fatigue life, shocks, dimensional tolerances, manufacturing feasibility, physical validation, or safety certification. Minimum element quality 0.096109 merits a local inspection. A controlled grid study, independent hand check, and common-reference load/moment audit remain outstanding.
